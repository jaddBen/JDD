Advice

What actually moves the needle

Ten things, roughly in the order they matter. The first three are worth more than everything else in this app put together.

01Turn up, for years

Three sessions a week for two years beats a perfect program you quit in March.

Nothing else on this list matters if you train for six weeks and stop. A session where you are tired and only manage 80% still counts. A session you skip does not.

When life gets in the way, shrink the session, don't cancel it. Do the first three exercises and go home. That keeps the habit alive, which is the actual asset.

02Add weight or reps, on purpose

Muscle grows because you keep asking it for more. If your bench press feels the same in June as it did in March, you will look the same too.

Double progression is the simplest way to run this, and it is why every exercise here has a rep range instead of one number:

  • Bench press says 6–8. Pick a weight you can get 6 with.
  • Stay on that weight until you hit 8 reps on every set.
  • Then add the smallest jump available — 2.5 kg on a barbell, one dumbbell size up.
  • You will drop back to 6 reps. That is correct. Climb again.

Write the weight in the app every session. You cannot beat a number you never recorded.

03Sleep is the actual supplement

7–9 hours. Not negotiable, and free.

You do not grow in the gym — you grow in bed. Training is the signal, sleep is when the building happens. Living on five or six hours blunts recovery, drops your strength on the big lifts and drives hunger up.

If the choice is an extra hour of sleep or a 6 a.m. session on four hours, take the sleep and train later.

04Leave one or two reps in the tank

Sets need to be genuinely hard, but grinding every set to total failure wrecks your recovery and makes everything after it worse, without building more muscle.

  • Big lifts — squat, bench, rows, presses: stop when you could have done 1–2 more clean reps. If your form is breaking, the set was already over.
  • Small stuff — curls, lateral raises, pushdowns, calves: take these close to failure. There is little to recover from, so chase the burn.

A rough test: if you could not have managed one more rep on a compound, you went too far. If you could have managed five, that was a warm-up.

05Eat enough protein, and enough food

Protein: roughly 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight, every day.

At 70 kg that is about 110–155 g a day, spread over 3–4 meals. Eggs, chicken, beef, fish, milk, yoghurt, lentils, chickpeas — whatever you will actually eat again and again.

Calories decide whether you gain or lose. Protein and training decide whether that change is muscle.

  • To build: a small surplus. Aim to gain about 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight per week — at 70 kg, roughly 200–350 g. Faster than that is mostly fat.
  • To lean out: a moderate deficit, losing about 0.5–0.75% per week. Keep protein high and keep training heavy so you hold onto the muscle.

Meal timing, shakes and anabolic windows are rounding errors next to hitting your daily totals consistently.

06Form first, ego last

The muscle cannot read the number on the bar. It only knows tension through a full range of motion. Half-repping 100 kg builds less chest than controlled full reps with 70 kg — and it is how people end up hurt and out for a month.

  • Control the lowering phase. A lot of the growth lives there.
  • Full range: bar to the chest, thigh below parallel, full stretch on curls.
  • If you have to swing it, bounce it or shorten it, the weight is too heavy.

Film a set from the side now and then. It is more honest than how it felt.

07Getting your first chin-up

This is what Day 3 is building toward:

Three things get you there, and you can run all of them at once:

  • Lat pulldowns — already the first exercise on Day 3. Same movement, same muscles, and you pick the weight. Keep adding to the stack. Once you can pull down roughly your own bodyweight for 8 clean reps, a real chin-up is usually there.
  • Negatives — jump or step to the top, chin over the bar, then lower yourself as slowly as you can. Aim for 5 seconds down, 3 sets of 3 at the end of Day 3. This is the single most effective piece.
  • Dead hangs — just hang for 20–30 seconds, twice. Grip is often the real limit, not the back.

Train it twice a week and most people get their first one inside 6–12 weeks. Being lighter helps too — it is your own bodyweight you are lifting.

No pull-up bar at all? A resistance band anchored high, or inverted rows with a barbell set at hip height, both build the same thing. Neither needs a special machine.

08What to actually expect

Training naturally, muscle comes slowly. These are honest ballparks rather than promises; genetics, age, food and sleep move them around a lot.

StageRealistic muscle gain
First year, trained well8–12 kg
Second year4–6 kg
Third year2–3 kg
After that1 kg or less

The fast strength jumps in the first few months are mostly your nervous system learning the lifts, which is why the bar climbs before you look any different. That is normal. Keep going.

Physiques that appear in twelve weeks online are either lying about the timeline or lying about the drugs. This program is built for the other route.

09Traps to avoid
  • Soreness is not the scoreboard. You can be wrecked by a session that built nothing, and grow from one that left you fine. Judge by the logbook.
  • Don't change program every three weeks. Give this 10–12 weeks before you judge it. Hopping between routines is the most common reason people stay the same size for years.
  • Don't skip legs. Day 2 sits in the middle of the week on purpose, so it is harder to dodge.
  • Take the full rest. The timer is not a suggestion — three minutes after a heavy squat set is what lets the next set be heavy too.
  • Deload when you stall. If the numbers go nowhere for two or three weeks and you feel beaten up, take one week at about half the sets. You come back stronger.
  • Pain is not soreness. Sharp, joint-centred or one-sided pain means stop that exercise and get it looked at. Training through it turns a week off into three months off.
10Every muscle, and what covers it

Counted straight from the program: direct is sets where the muscle is the target, assisting is sets where it helps. Roughly 10–20 direct sets a week is the useful range for a muscle you want to grow.

Three things are deliberately assistance-only. Your lower back works hard holding position on squats, Romanian deadlifts and rows — that is plenty, and adding direct work on top usually just costs you recovery. Obliques get the same treatment from lunges and the leg raise. Rear delts take a share of all four pulls on Day 3 plus the band pull-aparts in that day's warm-up, which is why there is no separate rear-delt exercise.

If you want them anyway, add either of these at the end of a day — they are optional, not missing:

  • Back extensions — 2×12–15, end of Day 2.
  • Side plank — 2×30 s each side, end of Day 2.
  • Rear delts — reverse pec deck or face pulls, 3×15–20, end of Day 3. Worth adding if your shoulders sit rolled forward.
  • Calves are on the low side at 4 sets. If yours are stubborn, add a second calf exercise to Day 2 for 3 more.

General training guidance, not medical or personal advice. If you have an injury or a health condition, get it checked before you start.
